# Field Research Documentation

## Overview

K-SETU includes a field-research feature in the admin interface to record structured observations about e-waste collection, aggregation, handling processes, awareness, and operational challenges.

The feature is intended to help organize field insights and inform future improvements to the platform.

> **Current status:** The entries included in the prototype are sample records. They must not be presented as actual interviews, verified field observations, or research findings.

## Accessing Field Research

In the admin interface, navigate to:

**Admin → Insights → Field Research**

The feature allows authorized users to review and record structured research notes.

## Information Recorded

The current research-note structure includes:

* **Participant reference:** A non-identifying reference used to distinguish a research entry.
* **General location:** Broad geographic context, rather than a precise private address.
* **Material:** The type of e-waste or material discussed.
* **Process:** Notes about collection, sorting, handling, or related activities.
* **Awareness:** Observations about knowledge of e-waste practices and recycling.
* **Challenges:** Reported or observed operational difficulties.

The intended structure avoids collecting direct personal identifiers in research notes.

## Sample Data and Limitations

The current field-research entries are marked as samples. They are included to demonstrate the feature and its data structure.

Sample records do not establish:

* The actual experiences or opinions of working collectors.
* The prevalence of a particular challenge.
* The representativeness of any location or participant group.
* Verified patterns in e-waste collection or recycling.
* The effectiveness or impact of the K-SETU platform.

Do not use sample entries as evidence of completed field research.

## Conducting Real Field Research

Before claiming that K-SETU has collected field data, the team must conduct real interviews with **at least two working collectors or aggregators**.

For each interview:

1. Explain the purpose of the research and how the information may be used.
2. Obtain appropriate informed consent before recording responses.
3. Use a participant reference instead of recording a participant's name in the research notes.
4. Record only information relevant to the research.
5. Use general location details and avoid unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
6. Distinguish direct participant statements from the researcher's own observations or interpretations.
7. Review the notes for accuracy and remove unnecessary identifying details.

The minimum of two interviews is a project requirement for beginning to report real field research. It does not, by itself, make the findings statistically representative or broadly generalizable.

## Privacy and Responsible Handling

Field-research information should be handled carefully:

* Avoid recording names, phone numbers, exact home addresses, or other direct identifiers in research notes.
* Collect only information needed for the stated research purpose.
* Obtain consent before conducting and documenting interviews.
* Restrict access to research records to authorized team members.
* Do not publish identifiable participant information.
* Store and share research notes using appropriate access controls.

Any future collection of personal information should be reviewed for appropriate consent, privacy, retention, and security safeguards.

## Using Research Insights

Once real interviews have been conducted and reviewed, the team may use appropriately anonymized findings to inform:

* Collector onboarding and user experience.
* Material classification and lot-creation workflows.
* Safety information and awareness content.
* Offline and low-connectivity requirements.
* Recycler discovery and transaction workflows.
* Future feature prioritization.

Clearly distinguish interview findings, direct observations, team interpretations, and hypotheses. Avoid claiming that a small sample represents all collectors or aggregators.

## Reporting Research Results

When presenting field-research results:

* State the number and type of participants interviewed.
* Describe the broad research context and limitations.
* Identify whether information came from interviews, observations, or sample data.
* Do not present synthetic entries as real findings.
* Avoid unsupported claims about the wider e-waste sector.
* Obtain permission before using quotations or other potentially identifying details.

## Current Project Status

The prototype demonstrates a structured field-research recording feature. Its existing entries are sample data. Real interviews with at least two working collectors or aggregators are required before the team claims to have collected field data.
